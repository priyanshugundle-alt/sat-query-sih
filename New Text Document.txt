These datasets collectively form the backbone of modern Remote Sensing Vision-Language Models (RS-VLMs) and Earth Observation AI.

Instead of traditional remote sensing tools that only output raw numbers, heatmaps, or segmentation masks, these datasets train AI models (like GeoChat, LLaVA-Med/Earth, and Remote Sensing GPTs) that allow humans to interact with satellite and aerial imagery using natural language.

Here is a breakdown of what each dataset is used for, why they exist, and how they combine into real-world applications:

1. Breakdown by Dataset & Specific Uses
🖼️ 

VRSBench/
 — Conversational RS-AI & Visual Grounding
Primary Use: Training Interactive AI Assistants (Chatbots for Earth Observation).
Key Capabilities Enabled:
Conversational QA (

VRSBench_train.json
): Models learn to hold multi-turn discussions about satellite scenes using LLaVA-style instructions (e.g., "What is the main activity in this industrial zone?" $\rightarrow$ "It is a container terminal with 3 gantry cranes and cargo stacks").
Automated Image Captioning (

VRSBench_EVAL_Cap.json
): Automatically generates text reports and summaries of reconnaissance images without human intervention.
Visual Grounding / Referring Expressions (

VRSBench_EVAL_referring.json
): Finds specific objects described in text and predicts their exact coordinates/bounding boxes (e.g., "Locate the white storage tank near the railway" $\rightarrow$ [x1, y1, x2, y2]).
🔄 

CDVQA/
 — Bi-Temporal Change Detection via Dialogue
Primary Use: Understanding what changed between two points in time using bi-temporal aerial imagery (before vs. after).
Key Capabilities Enabled:
Disaster Damage Assessment: Comparing pre-disaster and post-disaster images (e.g., "Have the residential buildings collapsed after the earthquake?" $\rightarrow$ "yes").
Urban Sprawl & Illegal Construction: Verifying if new structures or roads appeared over time without requiring an expert to manually compare gigapixel maps.
Environmental Degradation & Deforestation: Asking specific targeted questions: "Has the vegetated area on the riverbank shrunk?" $\rightarrow$ "yes".
🔭 

RSVQA-LR/
 — Macro-Scale Satellite Reasoning (Sentinel-2, 10m)
Primary Use: Global, large-scale environmental and land-cover analysis using freely available satellite imagery.
Key Capabilities Enabled:
Land Cover & Presence Verification: "Is there a water body present in this tile?", "Is arable land adjacent to forest?"
Regional Resource Monitoring: Because Sentinel-2 has a 5-day global revisit rate, models trained on RSVQA-LR can be continuously run over entire countries to track seasonal crop cycles, reservoir water levels, and drought patterns at 10m/pixel resolution.
🔬 

RSVQA-HR/
 — Micro-Scale Aerial Object Counting & Inspection (USGS, 0.15m–0.5m)
Primary Use: Fine-grained urban inspection and object counting from sub-meter aerial imagery.
Key Capabilities Enabled:
Exact Object Counting: Models learn to count distinct structures: "How many residential buildings are in this sector?" or "Are there between 5 and 10 vehicles in the parking lot?"
Municipal & Infrastructure Auditing: Checking building density, bridge conditions, road networks, and compliance with zoning laws.
🛰️ 

BigEarthNet.txt/
 + 

S1/
 / 

S2/
 — All-Weather Multi-Sensor Learning
Primary Use: Teaching models Radar (SAR) + Optical fusion.
Key Capabilities Enabled:
All-Weather & Night-Time Observation: Optical satellites (Sentinel-2) cannot see through clouds or at night. By pairing them with Radar (Sentinel-1 SAR VV/VH), models learn to understand ground conditions even under dense cloud cover.
Climate & Biome Reasoning: Connecting European climate classifications, geographical coordinates, and seasonal variations with spectral signatures.

When trained on this unified suite of datasets, a single Vision-Language Model can serve multiple industries:

Defense & Intelligence (GEOINT):
An intelligence analyst can prompt the AI: "Locate all surface-to-air missile launchers and report any new trench activity compared to last month." (Powered by VRSBench grounding + CDVQA change detection).
Disaster Management & Insurance:
Following floods or hurricanes: "List all submerged roadways and identify homes with missing roofs." (Powered by RSVQA-HR + CDVQA).
Agriculture & Forestry:
Monitoring carbon credits and compliance: "Has any part of this protected rainforest been cleared between March and September?" (Powered by CDVQA + BigEarthNet multi-sensor radar).
Smart Cities & Urban Planning:
Automated census of urban assets: "Count all commercial warehouses with rooftop solar panels in this district." (Powered by RSVQA-HR and VRSBench).

Recommended Model: EarthDial (CVPR 2025) backed by RemoteCLIP ViT-L-14

When fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL on satellite data, there are 4 very specific reasons why it often fails and gives wrong answers.

Reason 1: The "Black Image" Trap (Most Common in Remote Sensing)
Satellite imagery (like Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, or Cartosat) uses 16-bit GeoTIFF files where pixel values range from 0 to 10,000+.
Standard image libraries (like PIL or standard OpenCV) expect 8-bit JPEG/PNG images (0 to 255).
What happens: If the training script loaded the satellite image without doing 2%–98% percentile min-max normalization, the image passed to Qwen's vision encoder becomes completely pitch-black or corrupted noise.
The symptom: The model cannot see the image at all, so it guesses random things or hallucinates.
Reason 2: Training Loss on the Question Instead of Just the Answer
In standard instruction tuning, the model must only be penalized for what the Assistant says, not what the User asked.
If your training code did not set labels = -100 on the user prompt and image tokens:
The model tries to "predict" the question and image tokens instead of learning how to answer.
The symptom: The model repeats your question back to you, repeats the same word in a loop, or outputs gibberish characters.
Reason 3: Vision Tower Corruption (Over-aggressive Learning Rate)
In Qwen2.5-VL, the Vision Transformer (visual) is pre-trained.
If a training script applied a high learning rate (like $1 \times 10^{-4}$) to the vision encoder, it wipes out its pre-trained visual knowledge.
The fix: The Vision Tower should either be FROZEN or trained with a very low learning rate ($1 \times 10^{-6}$), while LoRA is applied strictly to the language attention layers (q_proj, v_proj, k_proj, o_proj).
Reason 4: Repetition Collapse on BigEarthNet
BigEarthNet.txt has millions of lines, but many are repetitive template text (e.g., "The land cover is Coniferous Forest").
If trained on raw repetitive lines without conversational diversity:
The model suffers from mode collapse—it learns to output the most frequent class (e.g., "Arable Land") for every single question, ignoring what is actually in the image.

Satellite GeoTIFFs (Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2) have a fixed North-Up geospatial affine matrix, but naive image loading can flip rows vertically.
Standard smartphone or drone photos contain EXIF rotation tags (0x0112); without handling them, images rotate 90° or 180° upon loading, throwing off bounding box coordinates.
The Code Fix:
We use rasterio.open() to maintain the exact top-to-bottom North-Up raster grid.
We added ImageOps.exif_transpose(img) to auto-align any smartphone, drone, or field camera photo to true upright orientation before tokenization.
Grounding bounding boxes are standardized to [ymin, xmin, ymax, xmax], matching Qwen2.5-VL's coordinate system.
2. Strict Anti-Data-Leakage Architecture (In 02_build_dataset.py)
The Problem (Spatial Autocorrelation Leakage):
In remote sensing, if you randomly shuffle question rows, Question A for patch 33UUP_69_78 might end up in Train while Question B for the exact same patch ends up in Val/Test. The model simply memorizes the scene rather than learning to reason.
If official benchmark test sets (CDVQA Test, RSVQA Test) are leaked into training, evaluation scores become invalid.
The Code Fix:
Geographic / Tile-Level Disjoint Splitting: We group by the Tile Patch ID (e.g., 33UUP_...). An entire satellite scene is placed either 100% in Train or 100% in Val—never in both.
Benchmark Test Set Isolation:
CDVQA Test (39k questions) and Test2 (31k questions) are kept completely out of the training loop.
RSVQA-LR/HR Test splits are strictly preserved for evaluation.
Only official Train splits are fed into satquery_train.json.

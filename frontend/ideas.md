# SatQuery AI Dashboard — Bright Investigator Mode Direction

## Three possible directions

### Theme Name: Solar Cartography
Very brief intro: A bright field-notebook workspace with warm paper surfaces, satellite-blue evidence zones, and citrus highlights. It makes complex remote-sensing analysis feel accessible, tactile, and optimistic.
Probability: 0.06

### Theme Name: Prism Observatory
Very brief intro: A high-key scientific dashboard where bold colour planes, translucent orbital marks, and animated evidence routes turn analysis into an energetic visual investigation. It feels contemporary and memorable rather than like a generic admin panel.
Probability: 0.04

### Theme Name: Terrain Pop
Very brief intro: A playful data-visualisation system inspired by cartographic posters and field stickers, using vivid green, coral, blue, and sun-yellow to make sensor evidence easy to distinguish at a glance.
Probability: 0.09

## Chosen approach: Prism Observatory

### Design Movement
Contemporary editorial data design fused with colourful scientific-explainer graphics. The direction borrows the confidence of modern cartographic posters, transparent film overlays, and youthfully optimistic research-lab identity systems.

### Core Principles
1. Make the investigation feel alive and approachable: use bright space, strong contrast, and a visible colour-coded evidence story instead of a dark command centre.
2. Organise the interface around one focal object—the active investigation—then let inputs, evidence, challenges, and verdict orbit it as connected layers.
3. Use colour semantically, not decoratively: blue for optical evidence, violet for SAR, lime for corroborated support, coral for challenge/review, and sun-yellow for next-best action.
4. Replace repetitive outlined cards with flat editorial planes, clipped labels, colour bands, and large breathable white space.

### Color Philosophy
The surface is a soft mineral white with pale sky-blue zones, so users immediately feel lightness rather than console fatigue. Cobalt blue anchors satellite observation, ultraviolet marks radar signals, acid lime indicates verified support, coral signals contradictions, and sunflower yellow carries helpful next actions. Deep navy is used only for readable text and critical structure.

### Layout Paradigm
An **Investigation Board** rather than a dashboard shell. A slim colour rail holds navigation; the main area opens with a bold mission banner and an asymmetrical three-part board: evidence inputs on the left, a large central satellite stage, and a bright verdict stack on the right. Below, a colour-coded evidence ribbon connects the analysis path from question to receipt.

### Signature Elements
- A large, soft-edged orbital halo behind the satellite canvas, made from translucent colour rings and map-grid dots.
- A colour-coded “case ribbon” that moves from hypothesis to evidence to verdict to next action.
- Bright sticker-like evidence labels with small sensor glyphs: blue for optical, violet for SAR, lime for support, coral for challenge.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like placing evidence on a field board. Scenario changes recolour the active investigation and update the related evidence chips. The Analyse action progresses the case ribbon, then reveals the verdict and next-best action. Every placeholder action gives a clear local-demo toast.

### Animation
Use short 160–240ms ease-out transitions for coloured evidence tiles, scenario tabs, and action buttons. When analysis starts, the case ribbon advances with a brief translate/opacity sequence; once complete, a lime verdict chip settles in. No continuous flashing. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System
Use **Manrope** for dense interface text and controls, paired with **Fraunces** for display headings and verdict statements. Technical labels use **IBM Plex Mono** at a small scale. Headlines are confident, colourful, and conversational; controls remain direct and compact.

### Brand Essence
SatQuery AI is a bright, evidence-first satellite investigation workspace for teams who want to understand what the imagery supports—and what to check next.

### Brand Voice
Headlines sound investigative and clear, never robotic. CTAs name the next field action rather than generic application actions.

Example lines:
- “Build the case from the ground up.”
- “Evidence is leaning toward water expansion. Add SAR to confirm.”

### Wordmark & Logo
A bold transparent mark combining a simplified sun-orbit ring with a square sensor aperture and a single coloured evidence dot. The mark should be graphic with no embedded text, clearly legible at favicon size and as a large workspace emblem.

### Signature Brand Color
**Prism lime: #B7F23A** — used for verified support, completed verdicts, and the most important active state.

## Style Decisions

- The dashboard stays explicitly labelled as Demo Mode until Java and a real model endpoint are connected.
- Evidence states must remain readable in grayscale through text labels and icons, not colour alone.
- The redesign uses an intentionally bright palette; deep navy appears only in type, outlines, and selective anchor surfaces.
- The layout must read as an investigation workspace, not a dark server/control-room dashboard.
- The active satellite observation is the opening focal object; input, verdict, challenge, and sensor evidence are supporting orbiting layers.
- Plain bordered cards are not the default container language. Prefer editorial colour planes, clipped labels, case ribbons, translucent overlays, sensor marks, and directional evidence routes.
- CTAs use investigation language such as “Test the case” and “Build evidence case,” not generic software labels.
- The case ribbon is the primary system motif: every major section visibly relates to the investigation path through directional routes, orbit marks, staged evidence labels, or connected colour bands.
- Plain white outlined cards are reserved for functional input areas. Explanatory, verdict, evidence, and readout areas should favour editorial colour planes, clipped labels, translucent overlays, or sticker-like tags.
- The satellite observation stage is the investigation nucleus, with surrounding inputs, challenges, verdicts, and receipts visually connected back to it.

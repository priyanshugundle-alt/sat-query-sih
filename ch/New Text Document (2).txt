Addressing Your Doubt: "Does Training on One Country Limit the Model?"
Your doubt is completely valid and intuitive: in BigEarthNet.txt, there are 10 European countries (Austria, Belgium, Finland, Ireland, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Portugal, Serbia, Switzerland, etc.), but our local S1/S2 patches are from Austria.

Here is why your model is not locked to one country, and how remote sensing physics works:

1. Physics & Sensor Signatures Don't Change by Country
Sentinel-2 Optical (Reflectance): Chlorophyll in plants absorbs red light (B04) and reflects Near-Infrared (B08). A cornfield or forest canopy has the exact same spectral curve whether it is in Austria, Germany, Finland, or India.
Sentinel-1 Radar (Backscatter): Radar measures surface physical properties:
Water acts as a mirror and reflects microwaves away from the satellite (dark pixels in VV/VH).
Buildings & Cities cause "double-bounce" reflections (bright white pixels).
Forests cause volume scattering (medium-bright textured pixels in VH). These physical radar principles are universal across the globe.
2. Why European Space Agency (ESA) Researchers Selected Austria
Austria was deliberately curated for BigEarthNet because it contains the most complex alpine topography, valleys, lakes, rivers, urban fabric, mixed/coniferous forests, and diverse agricultural fields in Europe. Learning on this terrain teaches the model the toughest landscape features.

3. How We Cover the Rest of the World (Phase 2)
The S1 & S2 training you just finished was the Foundation Phase (teaching the model dual radar + optical fusion).

Now, the remaining datasets in your workspace cover the rest of the world:

VRSBench: Covers dozens of diverse global cities, ports, airports, and rural areas from high-resolution satellites and drones.

1. What Does --grad-accum Actually Do?
In deep learning, the effective batch size is the number of satellite images the model inspects before calculating the gradient and updating its weights:

$$\text{Effective Batch Size} = \text{Batch Size per device (1)} \times \text{Gradient Accumulation Steps}$$

With --grad-accum 16 (Effective Batch Size = 16):
The model inspects 16 different satellite images across different tasks (e.g., 8 optical, 4 SAR radar, 2 bi-temporal change pairs, 2 urban bounding boxes).
It averages the gradients across all 16 diverse samples and takes one smooth, high-precision step.
Result: Higher stability, zero noisy oscillations, and higher accuracy across multiple satellite tasks.
With --grad-accum 8 (Effective Batch Size = 8):
The model updates weights twice as frequently, but each update only sees 8 images.
Trade-off: The updates can be noisier because satellite tasks (radar vs. optical) have very different loss surfaces.

Why These SSL Warnings Appear (and Why They Are Harmless)
Your college network uses a firewall/proxy with a self-signed SSL certificate that blocks connections to huggingface.co.

When PEFT tries to check for updates to the model config online:

It cannot connect to HuggingFace (blocked by college firewall).
It retries 5 times, then gives up.
It prints the warning "silently ignoring" and continues using the locally cached files instead.
The key phrase is:

silently ignoring the lookup for the file config.json

Meaning it already has everything it needs locally on disk at F:\Team Elite\hf_cache. It doesn't need HuggingFace at all.

Fix It Permanently (Prevent These Warnings in Future Chunks)
Run this once in your PowerShell before starting Chunk 3, 4, 5... etc:

powershell
$env:TRANSFORMERS_OFFLINE = "1"
$env:HF_DATASETS_OFFLINE = "1"
$env:HF_HUB_OFFLINE = "1"
This tells the entire library to never attempt internet access and always use local cache. No more SSL retries, no more warnings.
